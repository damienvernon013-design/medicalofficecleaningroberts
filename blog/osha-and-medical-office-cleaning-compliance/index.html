OSHA and Medical Office Cleaning Compliance

Cleaning plays a larger role in workplace compliance than most practice managers realize

OSHA general housekeeping standards

OSHA's General Industry Standards (29 CFR 1910.22) include housekeeping requirements applicable to every workplace: floors kept clean and dry, aisles and passageways kept clear, and drainage maintained in wet work areas. These apply to a medical office the same as any other facility.

The bloodborne pathogens standard

29 CFR 1910.1030 governs exposure to blood and other potentially infectious materials. It applies most directly to clinical staff, but cleaning crews working in exam rooms and dental operatories should understand basic exposure control concepts — what surfaces may be contaminated, and how to handle them safely without taking on tasks (like sharps disposal) that stay with clinical staff.

Documentation as protection

A medical cleaning company should be able to provide a cleaning log showing dates, tasks completed, and staff signatures, along with safety data sheets (SDS) for all products used. If your practice is ever audited or inspected, this documentation demonstrates proactive compliance — a far better position than having no records.

Cleaning as risk management, not just compliance

Beyond regulatory checkboxes, consistent cleaning reduces slip-and-fall incidents, chemical exposure events, and pathogen spread — each carrying both human and legal cost.
