Medical Cleaning Contracts: What You're Actually Paying For

The details buried in a cleaning contract determine what your practice actually receives

The scope of work section is everything

The most important part of any medical office cleaning contract is the scope of work. This section should detail every task the cleaning crew performs, how often, and in which areas of your facility — exam rooms, waiting room, restrooms, break room, and reception. Vague language like "general cleaning" is a red flag in a clinical setting. Look for itemized task lists broken down by room type, with specific mention of high-touch clinical surfaces.

Common line items in a medical office cleaning contract

  • Exam table, counter, and equipment surface disinfection between appointments or at end of day
  • Waiting room and reception area cleaning, including seating and high-touch surfaces
  • Restroom sanitization and restocking (paper, soap, sharps disposal awareness)
  • Trash and biohazard-adjacent waste handling protocols (not sharps handling itself — that stays with clinical staff)
  • Floor cleaning appropriate to clinical flooring (vinyl, sheet flooring, tile)
  • Glass, partition, and door hardware disinfection

What's usually not included

Most standard medical cleaning contracts exclude biohazard spill cleanup beyond routine surface disinfection, sharps container servicing, post-construction cleaning, and specialty equipment sterilization (that's the practice's own instrument reprocessing, not cleaning). If you expect anything beyond standard scope, ask for a supplemental rate schedule upfront.

Watch for these contract clauses

  • Auto-renewal clauses: Many contracts automatically renew for 12 months unless you give 30–60 days' written notice.
  • Price escalation clauses: Some contracts allow annual price increases tied to labor costs.
  • Termination fees: Leaving early may cost you several months of remaining contract value.
  • Complaint procedures: How do you report a missed clean, and what's the remedy timeline?

Always negotiate

Everything is negotiable, including trial periods and performance guarantees. A cleaning company with real clinical experience should be willing to customize the contract and scope to your practice's protocols.
